## Why Black Sabbath detuned their guitars
Guitarist Tony Iommi injured his fretting-hand fingers in a factory accident
before the band took off. Tuning down and using lighter strings made the
strings easier to press, and it also gave the band its heavy, dark sound.
Source: https://example.com/iommi-sound

## Describe the Eagles' vocal harmonies
The Eagles are known for tight, multi-part vocal harmonies, with several
band members singing lead and backing parts. Bands with a similar
country-rock harmony style include America and Poco.
Source: https://www.ethanelkind.com/the-eagles-repertoire-harmonies/

## What relatively niche musicians fit the shoegaze genre?
Shoegaze is built on walls of reverb-heavy guitar and soft, buried vocals. Good starting points beyond the most famous names include Slowdive, Ride, Lush, Swervedriver, and Chapterhouse, plus newer bands like Nothing and Whirr.

## Explain math rock.
Math rock is a style of alternative and indie rock with roots in bands such as King Crimson,
Yes, and Rush. It is characterized by complex, atypical rhythmic structures
(including irregular stopping and starting), counterpoint, odd time signatures, and extended chords.
Math rock has been classified as a derivative or subgenre of experimental rock,
sharing its avant-garde ethos and emphasis on through-composed structure rather than traditional song forms.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_rock

## Explain what the circle of fifths is and what is it used for?
In music theory, the circle of fifths (sometimes also cycle of fifths) organizes the twelve pitch classes of the chromatic
scale in a cycle of ascending perfect fifths. Since the cycle ascends in the opposite direction by perfect fourths,
it is sometimes also called the circle of fourths.  Chord progressions also often move between chords whose roots are related
by perfect fifth, making the circle of fifths useful in illustrating the "harmonic distance" between chords.
It is also used to organize and describe the harmonic or tonal function of chords. 
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_fifths
